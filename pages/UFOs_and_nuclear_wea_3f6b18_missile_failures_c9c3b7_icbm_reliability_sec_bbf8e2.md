## Introduction

Intercontinental ballistic missiles (ICBMs) are designed around a simple strategic promise: they must remain dependable enough that a nuclear command system can rely on them during the most extreme circumstances. Yet the history of ICBMs shows that reliability is not the same as perfection. Missile forces depend on thousands of connected components, communications links, power systems, software and human procedures, meaning a failure in a supporting system can temporarily remove missiles from alert without the weapon itself being damaged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span></span></span>

This issue became especially visible during Cold War incidents such as the 1967 Minuteman missile shutdowns at Malmstrom Air Force Base, Montana. ## Hidden dependencies inside missile systems

An ICBM is not an isolated rocket waiting in a silo. It is part of a larger network that includes launch control centres, underground cables, communication systems, guidance electronics, environmental controls, maintenance processes and trained personnel. A missile can become unavailable because one part of this network fails even when the missile structure and nuclear warhead remain intact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span></span></span>

This creates a central challenge for nuclear security: the system must be both highly reliable and highly controlled. Engineers cannot simply remove all complexity because nuclear forces require redundancy, communications, authentication procedures and safeguards against accidental or unauthorised use. Each added layer improves protection in one area while creating additional components that must themselves function correctly.

The Minuteman system illustrates this balance. The missile force was designed with hardened silos, underground launch control centres and multiple communication paths intended to preserve command capability. The Air Force describes Minuteman operations as relying on dispersed missiles, protected launch facilities and alternative command arrangements, including airborne systems that can assume control if ground links are lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span></span></span>

The same architecture that improves survivability can also make troubleshooting difficult. When several systems interact, identifying the source of a rare failure may require examining electrical conditions, hardware behaviour, maintenance history and human actions together rather than looking for one broken component.

## The Malmstrom shutdowns: a real failure behind a lasting controversy

The most famous example connected to nuclear-related UFO claims occurred at Malmstrom Air Force Base in March 1967. On 16 March, ten Minuteman I missiles in [Echo Flight]({{ 'echo-flight/' | relative_url }}) experienced a near-simultaneous loss of operational status, entering a “No-Go” condition. The missiles were temporarily unable to perform their alert mission, creating a serious concern because a group of strategic weapons had become unavailable at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaprad.org/cases/malmstrom-afb-missile-incident-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaprad.org">[UAPRAD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaprad.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD...</span></span></span>

The event later attracted attention because some personnel reported unusual lights or objects near the facilities. Former launch officer [Robert Salas]({{ 'robert-salas/' | relative_url }}) and other witnesses argued that the timing suggested a connection between the sightings and the [missile failures]({{ 'nuclear-missile/' | relative_url }}). However, the existence of the outage and the claim that an unidentified object caused it are separate questions. The missile malfunction is historically documented; a causal link to UFO activity has not been established by official investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaprad.org/cases/malmstrom-afb-missile-incident-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaprad.org">[UAPRAD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaprad.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD...</span></span></span>

Technical investigations focused on how a group of missile systems could enter a failed state. Later accounts of the Boeing investigation described attempts to reproduce the failure through an electrical signal introduced into parts of the control system. Supporters of a non-conventional explanation have argued that this raised questions about how such an effect could occur, while other analysts view the incident as an example of an unusual but conventional systems failure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/malmstrom67-2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">malmstrom67 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Malmstrom AFB Missile/UFO Incident, Mar 16, 1967...</span></span></span>

The significance for nuclear security is not whether the incident proves external interference. Its importance is that a highly protected strategic system experienced a failure mode that was difficult to immediately explain. Nuclear forces must be designed around the assumption that complex systems can fail and that operators need reliable procedures to diagnose and recover from unexpected conditions.

## Maintenance and reliability challenges

Keeping missile systems reliable over decades is a major engineering challenge. Nuclear forces often remain in service far longer than the original designers expected, requiring replacement parts, upgrades and specialised maintenance. The Minuteman III, introduced during the Cold War, has continued as a major part of the US land-based nuclear force through extensive sustainment programmes. The Air Force has acknowledged that maintaining hundreds of launch facilities and control centres requires long-term reliability-focused maintenance strategies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afmc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/1227531/programmed-depot-maintenance-a-major-milestone-in-minuteman-iii-sustainment/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afmc.af.mil">[Air Force Medical Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afmc.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Medical CommandProgrammed Depot Maintenance: A major milestone in Minuteman III sustainment &gt; Air Force Materiel Command &gt; Arti...</span></span></span>

Age introduces risks beyond the missile itself. Electronic components become obsolete, suppliers disappear, technical documentation becomes harder to maintain and older infrastructure may require increasing levels of specialist attention. Modernisation programmes are partly driven by the need to address these long-term reliability pressures while preserving nuclear readiness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/6212698/nuclear-missiles-icbm-triad-upgrade/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the $100 Billion Mission to Modernize America&#x27;s Aging Nuclear Missiles</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The fleet runs on technology that lags far behind modern standards, with maintenance costs rising to nearly $482 million annually. Reliability testing is therefore a continuous process rather than a one-time certification. Regular ICBM test launches are used to evaluate the missile, support equipment and personnel procedures under controlled conditions. The US Air Force states that these tests are intended to verify system readiness and reliability rather than demonstrate a response to immediate events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afmc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4498099/icbm-test-launch-verifies-system-reliability/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afmc.af.mil">[Air Force Medical Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afmc.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Medical CommandICBM test launch verifies system reliability &gt; Air Force Materiel Command &gt; Article DisplayMay 20, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 20, 2026</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that nuclear systems cannot be tested in the same way as ordinary equipment. Full operational scenarios are rare because the consequences of mistakes are unacceptable. Instead, reliability is built through layered safeguards, simulations, inspections, exercises and carefully controlled tests.

## Lessons for nuclear security

ICBM reliability failures demonstrate that nuclear security is not only about preventing an enemy from attacking a weapon. It is also about ensuring that the systems responsible for warning, communication and control behave predictably under stress.

One lesson is the importance of avoiding single points of failure. Redundant communications, backup command systems and strict maintenance procedures exist because any individual component may malfunction. The goal is not to assume that failures will never happen, but to ensure that one failure does not become a larger security crisis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force LGM-30G Minuteman III &gt; Air Force &gt; Fact Sheet Display</span></span></span>

Another lesson is the importance of distinguishing uncertainty from evidence. The Malmstrom incidents remain influential because they combine a confirmed technical anomaly with disputed interpretations about its cause. For nuclear security planners, the practical issue is not whether every unusual event has an extraordinary explanation, but whether systems can detect problems, recover quickly and prevent escalation caused by confusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uaprad.org/cases/malmstrom-afb-missile-incident-1967/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uaprad.org">[UAPRAD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uaprad.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Malmstrom AFB Missile Incident &#124; UAP Case Research &#124; UAPRAD...</span></span></span>

Cold War nuclear history contains other examples showing how technological faults can create danger without any physical attack. False warnings and computer-related errors in nuclear command systems demonstrated that reliability includes decision-making systems as well as missiles themselves. A failure in information flow can be as significant as a failure in hardware because leaders may have to make decisions under severe time pressure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newyorker.com/news/news-desk/world-war-three-by-mistake" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newyorker.com">[The New Yorker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newyorker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The New Yorker World War Three, by Mistake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The New Yorker World War Three, by Mistake</span></span></span>

The broader security lesson is that nuclear deterrence depends on confidence in complex systems. Reliability is achieved not by eliminating all possibility of failure, which is impossible, but by combining engineering discipline, maintenance, human judgement and safeguards designed to keep rare failures from becoming catastrophic events.

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
