## Introduction

The modern approach reflects a shift from the older UFO debate toward structured investigation. The United States Department of Defense (DoD) created the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) in 2022 to collect and analyse UAP reports across air, sea, space, and related domains. Its stated purpose is to reduce the risk of technological surprise by identifying and resolving unexplained incidents near national security areas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

## Why UAP became a defence issue

The national security relevance of UAP comes from a basic military principle: an unidentified object in controlled airspace creates uncertainty. Even when an incident is eventually explained as a balloon, drone, aircraft, [satellite]({{ 'satellite/' | relative_url }}), or sensor error, the initial inability to identify it can affect decision-making, force protection, and operational planning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

Modern defence concerns are therefore focused on several practical questions:

* **Could the object belong to an adversary?** Unidentified systems may represent foreign intelligence collection, unmanned aircraft activity, or emerging technologies.

## AARO’s modern investigation model

AARO represents the most significant institutional change in the modern UAP era. Rather than treating reports as isolated sightings, the office uses a data-analysis approach involving [military reporting]({{ 'military-reporting/' | relative_url }}) channels, sensor information, intelligence assessments, and scientific evaluation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

The office’s 2024 annual report showed the scale of the challenge. AARO received 757 UAP reports covering the period from May 2023 to June 2024, including both newly reported incidents and older cases submitted later. The report stated that many cases were resolved as ordinary objects such as balloons, birds, unmanned systems, satellites, or aircraft, while some remained unresolved because available data was insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

## Military encounters that shaped the modern debate

### The Navy encounters and the sensor problem

The modern public debate accelerated after several encounters involving US Navy pilots and military sensors. The 2004 USS Nimitz “Tic Tac” incident and later [Navy encounters]({{ 'navy-encounters/' | relative_url }}) became prominent because they involved trained personnel and multiple forms of observation, including aircraft systems and radar. These cases raised questions about whether some incidents reflected unknown technology, limitations in sensor interpretation, or incomplete information.

The significance of these cases for national security is not that they prove a specific explanation, but that they highlighted the difficulty of identifying objects operating near advanced military platforms. Military analysts must distinguish between unusual appearance, unusual behaviour, and genuinely unusual capability. A visual impression of extreme speed or manoeuvrability can be affected by distance uncertainty, camera systems, perspective, and atmospheric conditions.

## The role of drones, satellites and foreign technology

One of the biggest changes in modern UAP analysis is the recognition that many unidentified incidents may involve rapidly expanding human technology. Small drones, commercial satellites, electronic systems, and advanced aircraft can create observations that appear unusual without requiring unknown physics or non-human explanations.

This issue became particularly relevant as unmanned aerial systems became cheaper and more widespread. A drone near a military facility can represent a genuine security concern even if it is later identified, because the key question is who operated it and why. The same applies to foreign surveillance technologies designed to test military reactions or gather information.

Satellites also contribute to mistaken identifications. Research into aviation sightings has shown that satellite constellations such as Starlink can produce unusual appearances from aircraft cockpits, especially when reflected sunlight creates unexpected patterns. Such cases demonstrate why modern UAP investigation depends on combining witness reports with precise tracking data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08155" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Enhancing Space Situational Awareness to Mitigate Risk: A Single-Case Study in the Misidentification of a Recently-Launched Starlink...</span></span></span>

## Nuclear security and modern UAP assessment

Within the broader history of UFO and nuclear weapons reports, modern UAP investigations have moved away from claims that unknown objects are disabling nuclear systems and toward a more conventional security question: whether unidentified activity near strategic assets represents a vulnerability.

Nuclear facilities remain among the most protected locations in the world, and any unexplained activity near them receives attention because of the potential consequences. However, official modern assessments have not confirmed that UAP have interfered with nuclear weapons systems or demonstrated control over nuclear capabilities. Instead, the emphasis is on improving detection, reporting, and attribution around sensitive locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2024/4020-uap-2024?highlight=WyJmb3IiLCJhIiwiYSdzIiwiJ2EiLCJmb3IgYSJd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">4020 uap 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2024 Consolidated Annual Report on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena &#124; Office of the Director of National IntelligenceNovember 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. A drone intrusion, surveillance attempt, or sensor failure would be a serious national security issue even if it had an ordinary technological explanation. The security challenge lies in reducing uncertainty, not proving an extraordinary cause.

## What remains unresolved

The modern UAP question remains open in a limited but important sense. Governments have confirmed that some observations cannot immediately be identified, but unresolved cases represent a range of possibilities: insufficient data, unusual natural phenomena, classified technology, foreign systems, or ordinary objects viewed under difficult conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

The main evidence gap is data quality. Reliable identification requires accurate information about an object’s distance, speed, trajectory, environmental conditions, and sensor performance. Without those details, even trained observers can struggle to determine what they saw.

For national security agencies, the practical lesson is that better sensors, clearer reporting channels, and improved information sharing are valuable regardless of the ultimate explanation of individual cases. Modern UAP research has therefore become less about proving or disproving a single extraordinary theory and more about improving the ability of governments to understand unknown activity in critical domains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15368" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>
