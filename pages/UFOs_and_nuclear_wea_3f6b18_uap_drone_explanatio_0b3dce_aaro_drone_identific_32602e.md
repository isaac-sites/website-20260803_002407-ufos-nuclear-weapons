## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) approaches many UAP reports through a practical identification problem: determine whether an observed object is a known aircraft, drone, balloon, [satellite]({{ 'satellite/' | relative_url }}), natural phenomenon, or another explainable source before treating it as an unresolved anomaly. In the growing overlap between UAP investigations and drone concerns, the key challenge is not simply recognising that drones exist, but determining whether the available evidence is strong enough to attribute a sighting to a specific unmanned system. AARO’s reviews show that many resolved cases involve ordinary objects, including unmanned aerial systems (UAS), while other reports remain open because investigators lack enough reliable information about distance, speed, shape, sensor performance, or operating context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters in the [nuclear-security]({{ 'security-concerns-0719dd/' | relative_url }}) context because drones are a genuine technology and security issue. A drone near a sensitive facility may represent a conventional aircraft, an unauthorised operator, surveillance activity, or a misunderstanding caused by limited observation data. AARO’s work provides a framework for separating “unknown because the evidence is incomplete” from “unknown because the object demonstrates something beyond known technology”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Home...</span></span></span>

## AARO role in UAP investigations

AARO was established by the US Department of Defense to investigate unidentified anomalous phenomena using a data-driven approach. Its role is not to prove a particular explanation in advance, but to collect information, analyse reports, and determine whether cases can be attributed to known sources. AARO defines UAP as objects or events that are not immediately identifiable, meaning that “unidentified” describes the current state of knowledge rather than a confirmed type of object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Home...</span></span></span>

## Drone cases and attribution methods

### Why drones are difficult to identify

Modern drones create a particular challenge because they occupy a middle ground between simple aircraft and unfamiliar-looking objects. Small unmanned systems can hover, move slowly, operate at low altitude, carry cameras, and appear unusual when viewed from the ground or through limited-quality sensors. A distant drone may appear as a light, a dark shape, or a fast-moving object depending on lighting, camera type, atmospheric conditions, and observer perspective.

For investigators, the central problem is not whether a drone could produce a certain appearance, but whether the evidence demonstrates that a drone was actually present. A light seen in the sky without distance information cannot easily reveal whether it came from a small quadcopter nearby, an aircraft far away, or another source.

AARO case reviews show this distinction in practice. Resolved cases have included ordinary explanations such as balloons, birds, aircraft, satellites, and unmanned aerial systems. In its fiscal year 2024 report, AARO stated that it received 757 UAP reports during the reporting period and resolved cases to ordinary objects, including UAS, where sufficient evidence allowed attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/DOD-AARO-Consolidated-Annual-Report-on-UAP-Nov2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dni.gov.</span></span></span>

### The evidence chain behind identification

Drone attribution generally depends on combining several types of information rather than relying on a single image or witness description. Investigators may consider:

**[Sensor data]({{ 'sensor-data/' | relative_url }}):** Radar, infrared cameras, and other monitoring systems can provide measurements that help determine an object’s distance, movement, and physical characteristics.

**Flight information:** Known aircraft routes, drone activity, restricted airspace records, and operational data can eliminate possible explanations.

**Visual characteristics:** Shape, lighting patterns, movement, and behaviour can help distinguish drones from aircraft or natural objects, although visual evidence alone is often limited.

**Context:** Location matters. A report near a military base, airport, or nuclear facility receives additional scrutiny because drones in those areas may represent a security concern even when the technology itself is ordinary.

The challenge is that many UAP reports contain only part of this evidence chain. A video may show an object but lack range information. A radar track may show movement but not provide clear identification. A witness account may accurately describe what was seen while still leaving the object’s true size and distance uncertain.

## Why some reports remain unresolved

An unresolved AARO case does not necessarily mean investigators believe the object has extraordinary capabilities. In many cases, the unresolved status reflects missing data rather than unusual performance. AARO has stated that some observations remain unidentified because available sensors did not collect enough information for a definitive attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

Several recurring [evidence gaps]({{ 'evidence-gaps/' | relative_url }}) make drone identification difficult:

### Limited distance and scale information

Human observers often struggle to judge how far away an airborne object is. A small drone close to the observer and a large aircraft far away can appear similar. Without accurate distance measurements, estimates of speed and size can become unreliable.

### Incomplete sensor coverage

Many incidents are reported after the event, when investigators have only a short video clip or a written description. A longer recording showing approach, movement, and departure would often provide much stronger evidence.

### Changing drone technology

The rapid development of commercial and military drones creates an attribution problem. Investigators must continually update their understanding of what existing systems can do. A behaviour that appears unusual to an observer may be consistent with a newer drone design, unusual flight mode, or specialised mission.

### Security relevance versus technological mystery

Drone-related UAP cases are important even when they are resolved. An identified drone near a sensitive location raises questions about operator intent, security procedures, and airspace protection. The identification of the object answers one question — what it is — but not always another — why it was there.

This distinction is especially relevant around nuclear facilities. The Federal Aviation Administration restricts drone operations over certain critical infrastructure, including nuclear power plants, because unauthorised drone activity can create legitimate safety and security concerns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/uas/critical_infrastructure" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationCritical Infrastructure and Public Venues &#124; Federal Aviation AdministrationFebruary 22, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2023</span></span></span>

## What AARO reviews reveal about the drone explanation

AARO’s investigations have shifted the discussion away from a simple choice between “ordinary drone” and “unknown craft”. The evidence often falls into several categories:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Confirmed conventional objects:** enough information exists to identify the source, including some cases involving UAS.
* **Likely conventional but incomplete:** available evidence points towards ordinary explanations, but investigators cannot formally close the case.
* **Still unresolved:** insufficient information prevents a reliable attribution.

</div>

This framework is significant because it separates uncertainty from extraordinary claims. A case can remain unresolved without demonstrating impossible technology, just as a blurry photograph can remain unidentified without proving an unusual object exists.

For UAP reports connected to sensitive locations, including areas associated with nuclear security, the practical value of AARO’s approach is that it treats identification as an evidence problem. Better sensors, improved reporting procedures, and stronger links between military, aviation, and security organisations increase the chance that future drone-related observations can be classified accurately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

The continuing challenge is therefore not simply finding more explanations for UAP reports. It is improving the quality of the information available so that investigators can distinguish a drone, an aircraft, or another known object from a genuinely unresolved event.

      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
