## Introduction

Project Sign was the US Air Force’s first formal investigation into unidentified flying objects, created during the early Cold War when unexplained aerial reports were treated as a possible intelligence and security problem. Established in 1948 at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, it preceded Project Grudge and [Project Blue Book]({{ 'project-blue-book/' | relative_url }}) and created the investigative framework later used for thousands of UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/UFOs_and_nuclear_wea_3f6b18_declassified_documen_f0fd64_project_sign_origins_b72c79-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Project Sign illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/UFOs_and_nuclear_wea_3f6b18_declassified_documen_f0fd64_project_sign_origins_b72c79-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/UFOs_and_nuclear_wea_3f6b18_declassified_documen_f0fd64_project_sign_origins_b72c79-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The project is best known not because it proved any extraordinary explanation, but because it captured an important moment when military analysts were unsure whether unusual sightings represented Soviet technology, secret US projects, natural phenomena, or something genuinely unknown. Its surviving records and later accounts show a tension at the heart of early [UFO investigations]({{ 'ufo-case-investigation/' | relative_url }}): officials took some reports seriously enough to analyse them, while the evidence rarely supported firm conclusions about their origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

## Early Cold War concerns

### Why the Air Force created Project Sign

Project Sign emerged after the wave of “flying disc” reports that followed pilot Kenneth Arnold’s widely publicised 1947 sighting near Mount Rainier. In the aftermath of the Second World War, military planners were already focused on air defence, emerging aviation technology, and the possibility of foreign reconnaissance. Reports of unknown objects in American airspace therefore raised practical questions rather than only speculative ones: could another nation possess advanced aircraft, or were observers misidentifying ordinary events?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/project-sign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on disclosurearchives.com.</span></span></span>

The Air Force authorised a dedicated study under the Air Materiel Command at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. Initially associated with the name Project Saucer, the programme became Project Sign and began operating in early 1948. Its purpose was to collect reports, evaluate technical details, and determine whether unidentified sightings represented a potential national security issue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/project-sign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on disclosurearchives.com.</span></span></span>

This origin matters because Project Sign was not created as a public relations exercise or a search for evidence of [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) life. It was a Cold War intelligence-style assessment programme. Investigators examined witness descriptions, radar information where available, aircraft performance questions, and possible conventional explanations. The same approach later shaped the larger Air Force UFO archive, which included Project Sign, Project Grudge, and Project Blue Book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

## First Air Force assessments

### The unexplained cases that shaped Project Sign

Project Sign reviewed hundreds of reports during its short existence, but only a small number became historically important because they involved credible witnesses, military personnel, or unusual circumstances. These cases influenced how Air Force investigators viewed the limits of available explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoconnect.com/project-sign-and-project-grudge/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoconnect.com">[UFO Connect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoconnect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Connect Project Sign and Project Grudge Explained &#124; UFO Connect</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ConnectProject Sign and Project Grudge Explained &#124; UFO ConnectMarch 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2025</span></span></span>

One of the earliest major cases was the death of Kentucky Air National Guard pilot Captain Thomas Mantell on 7 January 1948. Mantell was sent to investigate a reported object near Fort Knox and pursued what witnesses described as a large metallic object. He climbed to a high altitude before his aircraft crashed. Later explanations considered possibilities including a misidentified astronomical object or a classified balloon project, but the incident became one of the defining unresolved cases associated with early Air Force UFO investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufoconnect.com/project-sign-and-project-grudge/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoconnect.com">[UFO Connect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoconnect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Connect Project Sign and Project Grudge Explained &#124; UFO Connect</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ConnectProject Sign and Project Grudge Explained &#124; UFO ConnectMarch 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2025</span></span></span>

The Mantell case demonstrated a recurring problem for Project Sign: investigators often had incomplete information. A sighting could involve trained observers and still lack enough measurements to identify the object conclusively. This uncertainty was central to the project’s work and explains why some reports remained classified as unidentified rather than being labelled as extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

### The Chiles-Whitted encounter

Another influential case was the July 1948 sighting by commercial airline pilots Clarence Chiles and John Whitted near Montgomery, Alabama. The pilots reported seeing a bright, fast-moving object pass near their aircraft. Because the witnesses were experienced aviators and described the object at close range, the case received significant attention from Project Sign investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiles-Whitted_UFO_encounter" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chiles-Whitted UFO encounter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chiles-Whitted UFO encounter</span></span></span>

The case became part of later debates over the so-called “Estimate of the Situation”, a reported internal Air Force assessment that allegedly considered extraterrestrial explanations among possible interpretations of UFO reports. However, the original document has not survived in the public record, and later explanations of the Chiles-Whitted event have argued that a meteor or bolide could account for the observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/project-sign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on disclosurearchives.com.</span></span></span>

The importance of Chiles-Whitted is therefore less about whether the object was unusual and more about what the case reveals about early UFO analysis. A sighting could appear highly convincing from a witness perspective while remaining scientifically difficult to resolve because of limited data on distance, speed, altitude, and atmospheric conditions.

## The disputed “Estimate of the Situation”

### What is known and what remains uncertain

The most famous controversy surrounding Project Sign concerns an alleged internal document known as the “Estimate of the Situation”. Later writers and former officials described it as a classified report that considered an extraterrestrial explanation for some UFO reports. According to these accounts, the estimate was rejected by senior Air Force leadership, but the document itself has not been located in surviving archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/project-sign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on disclosurearchives.com.</span></span></span>

Because the document is unavailable, historians treat claims about its contents cautiously. The surviving record confirms that Project Sign investigators were seriously examining unexplained sightings, but it does not provide a complete picture of internal debates or prove that the Air Force reached a conclusion about extraterrestrial origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosurearchives.com/project-sign" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosurearchives.com">[Disclosure Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosurearchives.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on disclosurearchives.com.</span></span></span>

The episode became significant because it highlighted a broader institutional challenge: military organisations had to investigate unusual reports without accidentally turning uncertainty into confirmation. The distinction between “unexplained” and “explained by extraordinary causes” became a recurring issue throughout later UFO programmes.

## Legacy of the initial archive

### How Project Sign shaped later UFO investigations

Project Sign lasted only until 1949, when it was replaced by Project Grudge, which later gave way to Project Blue Book. Although short-lived, Sign established the basic model of official UFO investigation: collect reports, evaluate evidence, separate probable explanations from unresolved cases, and maintain records for further analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

Its historical importance within the UFO and [nuclear weapons]({{ 'nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}) debate comes from the period in which it operated. The late 1940s were years of rapid military change, atomic weapons development, and anxiety about technological surprises. Some unusual sightings occurred near sensitive locations, but Project Sign’s surviving history does not show evidence that [unidentified objects]({{ 'unidentified-objects/' | relative_url }}) affected nuclear systems or that investigators confirmed a connection between UFO reports and nuclear weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

Project Sign remains valuable because it preserves the earliest official attempt to separate observation from speculation. The archive shows a government trying to understand reports that were sometimes puzzling, sometimes explainable, and occasionally left unresolved because the available evidence was insufficient. That unresolved category—not proof of an extraordinary origin—was the central legacy of the first Air Force UFO investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/754884/ufo-questions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
