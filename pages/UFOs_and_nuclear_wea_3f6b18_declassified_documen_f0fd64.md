## The archive that shaped modern UFO history

### Project Blue Book and the Cold War record

The largest and most influential declassified UFO collection is the US Air Force’s [Project Blue Book]({{ 'project-blue-book/' | relative_url }}) archive. Operating from 1952 to 1969, the programme collected and assessed thousands of reports of unidentified objects. After its closure, the records were transferred to the National Archives, where they became available for public research. ## What the documents reveal about nuclear-site cases

### Los Alamos and the “green fireball” investigations

One of the earliest nuclear-era UFO document trails concerns mysterious “green fireball” reports over New Mexico in the late 1940s. These sightings occurred near sensitive facilities, including the Los Alamos area, and attracted official attention because of concerns about unknown aircraft, [foreign surveillance]({{ 'foreign-surveillance/' | relative_url }}), or unusual atmospheric phenomena. The resulting investigations are important because they show how military agencies treated unexplained events around nuclear research areas as security questions rather than simply UFO mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

The surviving records do not show that investigators concluded the objects were [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) or that they affected nuclear operations. Instead, they illustrate the difficulty of interpreting unusual observations during an era of rapid technological change, when experimental aircraft, rockets, balloons, and atmospheric effects could all create unfamiliar signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

### Missile base reports and later interpretations

The most frequently discussed nuclear-related UFO documents involve alleged incidents at missile bases, particularly claims connected with Malmstrom Air Force Base in Montana in 1967. Former military personnel have described events in which unidentified objects were allegedly observed near missile facilities during periods when missiles experienced technical problems. These accounts became central to later arguments that UFOs had interfered with nuclear weapons.

However, the documentary record available to the public has not demonstrated that an unknown object caused those failures. The existence of [witness testimony]({{ 'witness-disputes/' | relative_url }}) and historical reports is different from proving a mechanism of interference. Reviews of government records, including later historical assessments, have not found confirmed evidence that UAP represented extraterrestrial technology or that such technology had affected nuclear weapons systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

## How to read a declassified UFO document

Declassified files can be compelling because they are official records, but their meaning depends on careful interpretation. A document showing that the government investigated a sighting proves that the report was considered worthy of attention; it does not prove the reported object was alien, advanced technology, or connected to nuclear weapons.

Several distinctions are essential:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **A recorded sighting is not a confirmed event.** Many documents preserve witness statements or preliminary reports rather than final conclusions.
* **An unidentified object is not automatically extraordinary.** “Unidentified” often means investigators lacked enough data to determine an explanation.
* **A classified document is not necessarily a hidden revelation.** Some records were protected because they involved military capabilities, intelligence methods, or unrelated sensitive information.
* **Historical archives contain uncertainty as well as answers.** Their value is often in showing how officials investigated ambiguous events rather than providing a final explanation for every case.

</div>

For nuclear-related UFO claims, the archive’s strongest contribution is therefore evidential: it allows researchers and the public to examine original records instead of relying only on later retellings. The documents confirm that unusual reports occurred around important military environments, while the available evidence remains insufficient to prove extraterrestrial involvement or direct interference with nuclear weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
