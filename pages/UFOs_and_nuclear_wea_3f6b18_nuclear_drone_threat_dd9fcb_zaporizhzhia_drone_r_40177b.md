## Introduction

The drone attacks and incidents around the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant (ZNPP) became one of the clearest examples of how modern warfare can create nuclear safety risks without a direct reactor strike. During the Russia–Ukraine war, drones turned the area around Europe’s largest nuclear power plant into a contested security environment where even limited impacts, near misses, or uncertainty over responsibility could increase the danger of escalation. The central concern has not been evidence of a nuclear accident caused by drones, but the way repeated drone activity can threaten safety systems, staff security, emergency planning, and confidence in the protection of a nuclear site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-warning-over-drones-near-nuclear-sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: world-nuclear-news.org">[World Nuclear News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">world-nuclear-news.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Nuclear News IAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Nuclear NewsIAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites - World Nuclear NewsMay 22, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2025</span></span></span>

The Zaporizhzhia case is important within wider discussions about unusual objects and nuclear facilities because it shows a shift from unexplained sightings to identifiable technology. Drones are real, accessible, and increasingly used in conflict for surveillance and attack roles. At a nuclear site, their significance comes from the combination of physical risk, uncertainty during military operations, and the possibility that a small incident could contribute to a larger crisis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03760" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected Areas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected AreasNovember 5, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2021</span></span></span>

## Drone incidents during the war

### The plant became a test case for nuclear security in active conflict

The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant has been under Russian military control since the early phase of the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Its location near active fighting has created an unusual situation: a major civilian nuclear installation operating in a battlefield environment. The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), which has maintained monitoring teams at the site, has repeatedly warned that military activity around the plant creates risks that nuclear facilities were not designed to manage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/iaea-team-ukraines-zaporizhzhia-says-it-heard-repeated-rounds-gunfire-2025-06-05/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IAEA team at Ukraine&#x27;s Zaporizhzhia says it heard repeated rounds of gunfire</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Zaporizhzhia plant, Europe&#x27;s largest with six reactors, has been under Russian control since early in the 2022 invasion of Ukraine an...</span></span></span>

Drone-related incidents have occurred at several points around the facility. In April 2024, the IAEA reported drone strikes at the plant, including impacts near reactor-related structures. The agency confirmed physical impacts but said there was no indication that critical nuclear safety systems had been damaged. It nevertheless described the incident as a serious escalation because attacks near nuclear facilities can increase the chance of a more damaging event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/a28710a691f3259b5dd6586787838b60" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The attack involved at least three direct hits on the reactor containment structures, escalating the risk of a major nuclear accident. Later incidents focused on the plant’s training centre, a building outside the main reactor perimeter but important for nuclear operations because it contains a full-scale simulator used for staff training. In January and May 2025, the IAEA reported drone-related explosions and impacts near the training centre. The May incident involved a drone hitting the roof of the building, with no casualties or major damage reported. The IAEA said repeated drone activity near nuclear sites should stop immediately because it could directly threaten nuclear safety and security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-warning-over-drones-near-nuclear-sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: world-nuclear-news.org">[World Nuclear News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">world-nuclear-news.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Nuclear News IAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Nuclear NewsIAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites - World Nuclear NewsMay 22, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2025</span></span></span>

Russia and Ukraine have issued competing claims about individual attacks, while the IAEA has generally focused on verifying impacts and warning about consequences rather than assigning blame. ## IAEA warnings and safety concerns

### Why a drone does not need to cause a meltdown to create a nuclear risk

The main nuclear safety concern from drones is not limited to the unlikely scenario of a single aircraft causing immediate reactor destruction. Nuclear safety depends on many supporting systems and human decisions: reliable power supplies, communication, trained staff, physical protection, emergency response, and the ability to maintain calm decision-making during a crisis.

A drone incident can increase risk in several ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **Damage to safety-related infrastructure:** A drone carrying explosives could damage equipment, buildings, or supporting systems even if reactor structures remain intact.
* **Disruption and uncertainty:** Operators and security teams may need to respond rapidly to unknown threats, increasing pressure on already stressed systems.
* **Escalation risk:** Military activity near a nuclear facility creates the possibility that a small event could trigger wider attacks or retaliation.
* **Security exposure:** Repeated drone flights can reveal information about site activity, response procedures, or vulnerabilities.

</div>

Research into drone protection systems has shown why these threats are difficult to manage. Small unmanned aircraft can be challenging to detect consistently because they are compact, manoeuvrable, and may require combined detection methods such as radar, optical sensors, acoustic monitoring, and radio-frequency tracking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03760" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected Areas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected AreasNovember 5, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2021</span></span></span>

At Zaporizhzhia, these problems are intensified by the wider condition of the plant. The IAEA has repeatedly warned that the facility’s safety margin has been reduced by the war, including fragile external power arrangements and the difficulty of maintaining normal operating conditions during conflict. Drone incidents therefore add another layer of pressure to an already weakened safety environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iaea.org/fr/themes/surete-securite-et-garanties-nucleaires-en-ukraine/mises-a-jour-du-DG" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iaea.org">[IAEA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iaea.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sûreté, sécurité et garanties nucléaires en Ukraine &#124; AIEA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sûreté, sécurité et garanties nucléaires en Ukraine &#124; AIEA...</span></span></span>

### The disputed question of intent

One of the most difficult aspects of the Zaporizhzhia drone incidents is separating capability from intent. A drone near a nuclear plant may represent reconnaissance, a deliberate attack, an attempted strike on military assets nearby, or an incident caused by battlefield activity. The immediate safety problem is that nuclear operators cannot assume the least dangerous explanation.

The IAEA’s response has focused on the principle that nuclear facilities should be protected regardless of who launches a drone or why it is present. Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi has repeatedly called for restraint around nuclear sites because even limited incidents can undermine nuclear safety and increase the risk of miscalculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/iaea-warning-over-drones-near-nuclear-sites" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: world-nuclear-news.org">[World Nuclear News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">world-nuclear-news.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Nuclear News IAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Nuclear NewsIAEA warning over drones near nuclear sites - World Nuclear NewsMay 22, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2025</span></span></span>

## Lessons for nuclear security planning

### Zaporizhzhia changed how planners view airborne threats

Before the war in Ukraine, nuclear security planning already considered aircraft crashes, sabotage, and unauthorised access. The Zaporizhzhia experience demonstrated that small drones create a different category of challenge: they are inexpensive, widely available, and capable of operating close to protected facilities.

The lesson is not that every drone near a nuclear site represents a catastrophic threat. Instead, the case shows that nuclear security must account for a wider range of airborne activity. Facilities need systems that can identify unusual drone activity quickly, determine whether it is accidental or hostile, and respond without creating additional danger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03760" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected Areas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Securing your Airspace: Detection of Drones Trespassing Protected AreasNovember 5, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2021</span></span></span>

IAEA teams provided independent observations during a period when competing military claims made information difficult to verify. ### The wider nuclear security takeaway

Zaporizhzhia became a defining example of how drones have changed the nuclear risk landscape. The most significant lesson is not that drones alone can easily cause a nuclear disaster, but that modern conflicts can place nuclear facilities under new forms of pressure. A drone strike, a near miss, or repeated flights around a plant can weaken safety confidence, complicate emergency response, and increase the chance of dangerous escalation.

For nuclear security planning, the case has turned drones from a theoretical concern into a demonstrated wartime challenge. The protection of nuclear facilities now requires not only strong physical barriers, but also better detection, clearer rules of engagement, international oversight, and safeguards designed for a world where small unmanned aircraft can reach sensitive sites.

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
