## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) historical review became the US government’s most detailed attempt to examine its own decades of UAP investigations and the claims surrounding alleged hidden programmes. Released in 2024, the review concluded that AARO found no evidence that any US government investigation had confirmed a UAP sighting as [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) technology and no empirical evidence that the government or private companies had reverse-engineered technology of non-human origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

The report did not declare every UAP case solved. Instead, its central finding was narrower: after reviewing historical investigations, records, and allegations of concealed technology programmes, AARO found that the available evidence did not support claims of recovered alien craft, secret extraterrestrial engineering efforts, or confirmed links between UAP and advanced non-human technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?ver=WwWcCg5XNKBtz9PnBXMI8A%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span> This distinction matters in debates about [UFO transparency]({{ 'government-ufo/' | relative_url }}), especially claims involving sensitive military sites and [nuclear weapons]({{ 'nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}), because AARO’s review focused on what historical evidence could actually demonstrate rather than what remained unexplained.

## Creation of AARO and its role

AARO was created as the Department of Defense office responsible for coordinating UAP investigations across military and intelligence organisations. Its purpose was to replace a fragmented system of investigations with a central office able to collect reports, analyse evidence, and provide assessments to government leaders and Congress. The historical review was one of its first major efforts to establish a baseline of what the US government had investigated since the beginning of the modern UFO era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

The historical review was broader than a review of recent military encounters. AARO examined government efforts dating back to 1945, including programmes such as Project SIGN, Project GRUDGE, [Project BLUE BOOK]({{ 'project-blue-book/' | relative_url }}), later studies connected to UAP, and more recent military efforts including the UAP Task Force. It also reviewed claims made by individuals who alleged that the government or contractors had recovered non-human technology and concealed it from public oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

AARO’s approach was significant because it treated UAP disclosure claims as an evidence-review problem. Rather than asking only whether unusual sightings existed, the office examined whether historical records supported specific claims: that governments possessed extraterrestrial materials, that secret [reverse-engineering]({{ 'reverse-engineering/' | relative_url }}) programmes existed, or that official investigations had confirmed an extraordinary origin for any sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

## Historical investigations and findings

### What AARO found across decades of UAP research

The central conclusion of Volume I was that no US government investigation, academic-sponsored study, or [official review]({{ 'official-review/' | relative_url }}) panel had confirmed that a UAP represented extraterrestrial technology. AARO also found no empirical evidence supporting claims that the US government or private industry had reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

The review examined why UAP reports have persisted despite decades of investigation. AARO found that many cases could be explained by ordinary objects or phenomena, including misidentified aircraft, balloons, satellites, natural events, and sensor or perception errors. Some cases remained unresolved, but AARO attributed many unresolved cases to limitations in available information rather than evidence of extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/27" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/27 - Wikisource, the free online libraryMay 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2024</span></span></span>

This finding addressed a common misunderstanding in UAP debates: an unresolved case is not the same as evidence of an alien spacecraft. AARO’s review treated “unidentified” as a temporary status indicating insufficient information, not as proof of a specific explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/27" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/27 - Wikisource, the free online libraryMay 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2024</span></span></span>

### The review of alleged hidden technology programmes

One of the most important parts of AARO’s work involved claims that the US government had recovered crashed non-human vehicles and operated secret programmes to study or reproduce their technology. AARO stated that it found no verifiable evidence supporting these claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

The office assessed that some alleged hidden programmes either did not exist, were based on misunderstandings, or involved real national security programmes that were unrelated to extraterrestrial technology. AARO also examined claims involving supposed recovered materials and concluded that the material evidence available did not demonstrate an off-world origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/10 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

### Why nuclear-related UAP claims remained part of the wider debate

Although AARO’s historical review was not solely a report about nuclear weapons, it was relevant to claims involving nuclear facilities because many prominent UFO stories have centred on military installations and strategic assets. These claims often involve allegations that unknown objects interfered with, monitored, or appeared near nuclear systems.

AARO’s broader historical finding was that it did not identify evidence proving extraterrestrial technology or hidden alien programmes behind such claims. The report therefore shifted the [transparency]({{ 'transparency/' | relative_url }}) debate away from proving extraordinary events and towards questions about records, data quality, classification, and whether government investigations have enough information to resolve unexplained cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

## Limits and remaining disclosure debates

AARO’s findings resolved some questions while leaving others open. The report’s conclusion was about evidence available in government records and investigations, not a declaration that every UAP report has been explained. Some cases remain unresolved because investigators lack sufficient sensor data, reliable measurements, or complete historical documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/27" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/27 - Wikisource, the free online libraryMay 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The main continuing debate concerns whether unresolved cases reflect ordinary information gaps or whether important evidence remains unavailable because of classification. Critics of AARO’s conclusions argue that incomplete access to historical records could limit what any review can determine. Supporters of AARO argue that extraordinary claims require equally strong evidence and that decades of official investigations have not produced verified proof of extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thetimes.co.uk/article/ufo-hearing-congress-uap-witnesses-q6sq0z56b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thetimes.co.uk">[The Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thetimes.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times UFO hearing: Congress tells government to come clean on what it knows</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times UFO hearing: Congress tells government to come clean on what it knows</span></span></span>

The significance of the historical review is therefore not that it ended the UAP debate, but that it established a clearer evidentiary standard. AARO’s review found no confirmed extraterrestrial technology, no verified reverse-engineering programme, and no historical record showing that official investigations had established an alien origin for UAP. At the same time, it reinforced the need for better data collection and transparent review processes when future unexplained incidents occur.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>
