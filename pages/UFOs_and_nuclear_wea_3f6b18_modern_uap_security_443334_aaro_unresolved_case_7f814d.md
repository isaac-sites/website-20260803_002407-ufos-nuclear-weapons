## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) was created to bring a structured intelligence and scientific process to modern UAP investigations. Its role is not to prove or disprove extraordinary explanations for every sighting, but to determine what reported objects are, whether they represent a national security concern, and why some cases cannot yet be identified. AARO’s central finding is that many UAP reports can be resolved as ordinary objects or sensor-related issues, while a smaller group remains unresolved because the available evidence is incomplete rather than because an unusual explanation has been confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>

This distinction is especially important in discussions involving sensitive locations, including military operating areas and sites associated with strategic capabilities. An unresolved UAP case is treated as an information gap: an object has not been confidently attributed, and investigators need better data before reaching a conclusion. AARO’s work shows how governments attempt to separate genuine security questions from cases where uncertainty is caused by limited observations, missing sensor records, or insufficient detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3100053/dod-announces-the-establishment-of-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDoD Announces the Establishment of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; ReleaseJ...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3100053/dod-announces-the-establishment-of-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office/)

## AARO’s mission and reporting system

AARO, established by the U.S. Department of Defense in 2022, serves as the primary US government office responsible for analysing UAP reports across defence and intelligence channels. Its mission is to reduce the risk of technological surprise by improving the detection, identification, attribution, and management of [unidentified objects]({{ 'unidentified-objects/' | relative_url }}) near national security areas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>

Rather than treating each sighting as an isolated mystery, AARO uses a system based on data collection and analysis. Reports may include information from military personnel, aircraft sensors, intelligence sources, and other government channels. The office also receives relevant aviation reports through cooperation with civilian authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Home...</span></span></span>

The reporting process reflects a key intelligence principle: an unidentified object is not automatically an unknown technology. A pilot, sensor operator, or military unit may observe something that appears unusual, but investigators need enough information to determine whether the cause was an aircraft, balloon, drone, [satellite]({{ 'satellite/' | relative_url }}), atmospheric effect, sensor limitation, or something else. AARO has repeatedly emphasised that many unresolved cases remain open because the evidence does not allow a reliable attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/FAQ/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO FAQ...</span></span></span>

AARO’s public reporting has also changed how UAP cases are presented. Instead of a simple explained-versus-unexplained divide, cases are assessed according to the quality of evidence and the confidence of the conclusion. A case may remain unresolved even when investigators believe it is likely ordinary, because intelligence standards require evidence strong enough to support a formal determination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## Resolved cases versus unresolved incidents

AARO’s investigations have produced a pattern that differs from many popular assumptions about UAP. The majority of cases that can be closed are attributed to conventional explanations, including balloons, birds, drones, aircraft, satellites, and other identifiable objects. The unresolved category largely represents cases where investigators cannot complete that final identification step.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3100053/dod-announces-the-establishment-of-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDoD Announces the Establishment of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; ReleaseJ...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

The scale of the review illustrates the challenge. In its 2024 annual report covering incidents reported between May 2023 and June 2024, AARO received 757 UAP reports. Of these, 485 involved incidents during the reporting period, while 272 were older incidents submitted later. The total number of cases under review exceeded 1,600.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3100053/dod-announces-the-establishment-of-the-all-domain-anomaly-resolution-office/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDoD Announces the Establishment of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; ReleaseJ...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964824/department-of-defense-releases-the-annual-report-on-unidentified-anomalous-phen/)

AARO’s unresolved cases are therefore not a single category with a single meaning. They include different situations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Insufficient observation:** A video or witness report may show an object but lack distance, speed, altitude, or location data needed for identification.
* **Limited sensor information:** Infrared footage or radar data may reveal an object’s presence without providing enough detail about its characteristics.
* **Incomplete context:** Investigators may need flight records, satellite data, weather information, or operational details that are unavailable.
* **Cases requiring further analysis:** Some incidents may contain features that justify additional review without indicating a confirmed extraordinary capability.

</div>

A useful example is AARO’s treatment of official imagery. Some released cases remain unresolved not because investigators concluded that an object demonstrated impossible performance, but because the available footage did not provide enough information for a definitive identification. In other cases, additional analysis has allowed AARO to identify ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

This approach also applies to well-known military UAP incidents. Public attention has often focused on videos such as the “GoFast”, “Gimbal”, and other Navy-related recordings. AARO’s broader investigation framework treats such cases as evidence problems requiring technical assessment rather than as automatic evidence of advanced technology. The question is whether the available data supports a conclusion, not whether the footage appears unusual to viewers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-case-resolution-reports-overview/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uap-archive.org.</span></span></span>

## Why missing data limits conclusions

The biggest factor behind unresolved UAP cases is not a lack of interest from investigators but a lack of high-quality information. AARO has stated that many cases cannot be resolved because they lack verifiable data needed for scientific and intelligence analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission Brief...</span></span></span>

Modern military sensors are powerful, but they are designed for specific missions. A camera optimised for detecting heat may not provide accurate size or distance measurements. A radar return may show movement but not identify the object’s physical characteristics. A pilot’s visual estimate may be valuable but can be affected by altitude, lighting, perspective, and atmospheric conditions.

This creates a central challenge: unusual appearance does not always equal unusual capability. A small object viewed through a limited sensor system may appear to move rapidly or behave strangely because of perspective effects, tracking limitations, or incomplete information. Resolving such cases requires multiple data sources that can be compared against one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission Brief...</span></span></span>

AARO has also identified collection bias as a limitation. Because many reports originate from defence sources, its database naturally contains more incidents near military ranges, operational areas, and locations where surveillance activity is higher. This means the distribution of reports reflects where sensors and trained observers are concentrated, not necessarily where unusual objects occur most often.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Mission_Brief_2025.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission Brief</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission Brief...</span></span></span>

## What unresolved UAP cases actually show

The unresolved cases are significant because they reveal a practical national security problem: uncertainty itself can matter. A military organisation must understand unidentified objects near sensitive areas even when the final explanation is ordinary. A drone, balloon, [foreign surveillance]({{ 'foreign-surveillance/' | relative_url }}) platform, or sensor error can each require different responses.

At the same time, AARO’s findings do not show that unresolved cases are evidence of [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) technology or confirmed breakthrough systems. The office’s historical review found no verified evidence that UAP investigations had confirmed extraterrestrial technology or recovered non-human craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

The value of AARO’s work is therefore in improving attribution. A resolved case reduces uncertainty by identifying what happened. An unresolved case identifies where better information is needed. For governments concerned with airspace security and strategic facilities, the important outcome is not simply a larger catalogue of sightings, but a more reliable process for distinguishing genuine threats from ordinary events that initially appear unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/About/Mission-Vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Mission/Vision...</span></span></span>
