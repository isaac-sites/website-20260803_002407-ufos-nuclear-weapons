## Introduction

The Los Alamos green fireball investigations were one of the earliest Cold War efforts to examine unusual aerial sightings near America’s nuclear research complex. Beginning in the late 1940s, witnesses in New Mexico reported bright green objects moving across the night sky near Los Alamos, Sandia Base, and other sensitive installations. The reports attracted attention not because officials accepted an extraordinary explanation, but because unexplained events near atomic facilities created a genuine security concern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-green-fireballs-nuclear-facilities-new-mexico" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos green fireballs nuclear facilities new mexico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Mysterious Green Fireballs Worried the US Government &#124; HISTORYAugust 17, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 2018</span></span></span>

The investigations became a landmark example of how the US government handled unidentified aerial reports during the nuclear age. Scientists, military officers, and intelligence personnel examined whether the fireballs were natural meteors, secret technology, [foreign surveillance]({{ 'foreign-surveillance/' | relative_url }}), or another unknown phenomenon. The surviving records show serious investigation and uncertainty, but they do not prove that the objects were [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) or that they affected nuclear weapons operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://warufo.com/document/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: warufo.com">[WARUFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">warufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DOE-UAP-D004, Los Alamos Conference on Aerial Phenomena, 1949 — WARUFO — Declassified UAP Document ArchiveJuly 10, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 10, 2026</span></span></span>

## Cold War sightings near nuclear research

### Why Los Alamos became the centre of attention

The green fireball reports emerged during a period when New Mexico had become one of the most security-sensitive regions in the United States. Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory was central to nuclear weapons research, while nearby facilities such as Sandia Base supported atomic weapons development. [Unidentified objects]({{ 'unidentified-objects/' | relative_url }}) appearing over this landscape naturally raised questions about possible surveillance, experimental aircraft, or security threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-green-fireballs-nuclear-facilities-new-mexico" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos green fireballs nuclear facilities new mexico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Mysterious Green Fireballs Worried the US Government &#124; HISTORYAugust 17, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 2018</span></span></span>

Reports increased during 1948 and early 1949. Witnesses commonly described brilliant green lights or fireballs moving quickly across the sky, sometimes appearing to travel on unusual trajectories. Some observers included military personnel and scientists, which helped convince officials that the reports deserved investigation rather than simple dismissal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The significance of the case came from the combination of three factors:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Location:** sightings occurred close to nuclear laboratories and military installations.
* **Appearance:** witnesses described unusually bright green objects unlike ordinary aircraft.
* **Uncertainty:** investigators lacked enough measurements to identify the phenomenon confidently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

</div>

The concern was therefore primarily about information gaps. In a period of intense rivalry with the Soviet Union, an unexplained object near strategic facilities represented a question for intelligence and scientific analysis.

## Official investigations and possible explanations

### The 1949 Los Alamos scientific meeting

A major turning point was a 1949 conference at Los Alamos that brought together military representatives and scientists to examine the green fireball reports. Participants discussed possible explanations and the need for better observations, including photography, measurements, and trajectory analysis. The available record shows that no single explanation was accepted at the meeting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://warufo.com/document/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: warufo.com">[WARUFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">warufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DOE-UAP-D004, Los Alamos Conference on Aerial Phenomena, 1949 — WARUFO — Declassified UAP Document ArchiveJuly 10, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 10, 2026</span></span></span>

Scientists considered several possibilities:

* **[Meteor explanations]({{ 'meteor-explanations/' | relative_url }}):** Some researchers argued that unusual meteors entering the atmosphere could account for the colour, brightness, and apparent movement. Green coloration is possible when certain elements in meteors produce light during atmospheric entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/rufos4u.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: The Report On Unidentified Flying Objects - Chapter Four Unedited...</span></span></span>
* **Atmospheric or optical effects:** Investigators considered whether unusual atmospheric conditions could distort perceptions of speed, direction, or distance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>
* **Artificial causes:** Because of the location near nuclear facilities, officials also considered whether the sightings could involve foreign technology or classified activities. The investigations did not establish such a cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-green-fireballs-nuclear-facilities-new-mexico" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos green fireballs nuclear facilities new mexico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Mysterious Green Fireballs Worried the US Government &#124; HISTORYAugust 17, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 17, 2018</span></span></span>

One important figure in the investigation was astronomer Lincoln LaPaz, an expert in meteoritics who worked with military investigators. LaPaz and other scientists were interested in the problem because the observations did not always match familiar meteor behaviour, particularly when considering witness descriptions of trajectories and appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

### Project Twinkle: trying to measure the unknown

The Air Force later created Project Twinkle, an attempt to obtain scientific observations of the green fireballs using dedicated equipment. The project aimed to replace eyewitness accounts with measurable data, including photographic and instrumental observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

Project Twinkle illustrates the practical difficulty of investigating unusual aerial reports. Researchers needed precise information about speed, altitude, brightness, and trajectory, but many sightings were brief and unpredictable. The final investigation concluded that the available evidence did not demonstrate an extraordinary explanation and suggested that natural causes were likely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/twinkle.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Casebook Project Twinkle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Casebook Project Twinkle</span></span></span>

The project was not a simple dismissal of the sightings. Rather, it represented an attempt to convert uncertain observations into scientific measurements. The difficulty was that the best observations were often made by individual witnesses rather than instruments designed for the task.

## What records do and do not prove

### What the documents establish

The declassified record establishes several important points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Government agencies took the sightings seriously enough to investigate them.
* Nuclear-related facilities made unusual aerial reports a security issue.
* Scientists and military officials discussed the sightings openly within official channels.
* Investigators explored ordinary and extraordinary explanations rather than assuming one answer in advance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

</div>

This makes the green fireball case historically important. It shows that early [UFO investigations]({{ 'ufo-case-investigation/' | relative_url }}) were not only about public curiosity; they were also connected to Cold War concerns about airspace, technology, and intelligence.

### What the records do not prove

The documents do not demonstrate that the green fireballs were alien spacecraft, secret enemy weapons, or devices connected to nuclear systems. They also do not show that the objects interfered with atomic research or weapons operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The strongest conclusion supported by the evidence is more limited: officials encountered a genuine observational problem near strategically important sites and attempted to solve it with the tools available at the time. The remaining uncertainty reflects incomplete data rather than confirmation of an extraordinary explanation.

The Los Alamos green fireball investigations therefore occupy an unusual place in UFO history. They were not significant because they proved a hidden technology or extraterrestrial presence, but because they reveal how Cold War institutions responded when unexplained events appeared close to the centre of America’s nuclear programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
