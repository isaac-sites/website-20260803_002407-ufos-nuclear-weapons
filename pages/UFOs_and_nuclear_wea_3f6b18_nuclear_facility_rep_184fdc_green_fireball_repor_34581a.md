## Introduction

The “green fireball” reports of the late 1940s were among the earliest Cold War aerial mysteries to attract serious attention from [scientists]({{ 'scientists/' | relative_url }}) and military officials. Beginning in 1948, unusually bright green lights were repeatedly reported over the American Southwest, especially near sensitive atomic research locations such as Los Alamos and Sandia. The events alarmed researchers because they occurred close to nuclear facilities, appeared unlike ordinary aircraft, and sometimes seemed inconsistent with familiar meteor behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

Official investigations eventually favoured natural explanations, particularly unusual meteoric phenomena, while acknowledging that some observations lacked enough data for immediate identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/twinkle/ProjectTwinkleFinalReport.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Twinkle Final Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I &amp; B &gt; 2 P 5* o 0. » UNCLASSIFIED c&gt;...</span></span></span>

## Early sightings over the Southwest

The green fireball episode emerged during a period when atomic secrecy and Cold War anxiety made unusual activity near research sites especially significant. The first major reports appeared in the skies of New Mexico in 1948, including sightings by pilots, military personnel and scientists. The region was strategically important because it contained the Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory, where [nuclear weapons]({{ 'nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}) research had begun during the Manhattan Project, and Sandia facilities involved in weapons development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span></span></span>

Witnesses commonly described the objects as intense green lights moving rapidly across the sky, often without the visible trails or sounds associated with conventional aircraft. Some reports described them as travelling in a shallow path rather than dropping steeply like many meteors. The unusual colour was one reason the events attracted attention: a bright green glow was uncommon enough to raise questions among observers familiar with ordinary sky phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/rufos4u.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: The Report On Unidentified Flying Objects - Chapter Four Unedited...</span></span></span>

One important feature of the reports was the quality of some witnesses. These were not simply anonymous accounts from distant observers; military personnel, pilots and scientists also reported seeing unusual lights. The Air Force’s later historical summary notes that a 1948 investigation involved airline pilots reporting green fireballs over the Southwest, with investigators consulting meteor expert Dr Lincoln La Paz of the University of New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span></span></span>

The proximity to atomic sites changed the way officials treated the sightings. A strange light over a remote area might have been dismissed as a meteor or aircraft, but a recurring phenomenon near laboratories connected to nuclear research raised security questions. Officials had to consider whether the sightings represented unknown natural events, experimental technology, or possible [foreign surveillance]({{ 'foreign-surveillance/' | relative_url }}) during an increasingly tense international environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

## Scientific and military investigations

The response to the green fireballs was unusually serious for the era. In 1949, officials and scientists met at Los Alamos to discuss the observations, bringing together representatives connected with military intelligence, nuclear research and atmospheric science. The discussions included figures such as physicist Edward Teller and meteor expert Lincoln La Paz, reflecting the level of concern generated by the reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://warufo.com/document/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: warufo.com">[WARUFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">warufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DOE-UAP-D004, Los Alamos Conference on Aerial Phenomena, 1949 — WARUFO — Declassified UAP Document ArchiveJuly 10, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 10, 2026</span></span></span>

La Paz became one of the central scientific figures in the investigation. He examined whether the sightings matched known meteor behaviour and highlighted several difficulties: some reported trajectories appeared unusually shallow, the colours seemed unusual, and investigators did not always find expected meteorite material. These points led some researchers to consider whether the phenomenon might involve something other than ordinary meteors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/rufos4u.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: The Report On Unidentified Flying Objects - Chapter Four Unedited...</span></span></span>

However, uncertainty did not mean officials concluded the objects were artificial. The challenge was that observations made by human witnesses alone could not provide reliable measurements of altitude, speed, size or composition. Investigators concluded that better instruments were needed, including photography, triangulation and spectroscopy — methods that could turn eyewitness impressions into measurable data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/twinklereport.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Project Twinkle: Final ReportNovember 27, 1951...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 27, 1951</span></span></span>

This led to Project Twinkle, a formal effort to study the phenomenon. Established under the Air Force’s Geophysics Research Division, the project attempted to capture and analyse future sightings. The final report concluded that the observations studied were most consistent with known categories such as meteors, aircraft, balloons and other conventional phenomena, and stated that there was no indication the green fireballs represented anything beyond natural phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/twinkle/ProjectTwinkleFinalReport.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Twinkle Final Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I &amp; B &gt; 2 P 5* o 0. » UNCLASSIFIED c&gt;...</span></span></span>

Project Twinkle’s limitations became part of the historical debate. The investigation struggled with practical problems: sightings were unpredictable, equipment was limited, and observers often arrived after events had already ended. The project did not produce the kind of photographic or instrumental evidence investigators had hoped would settle the question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ARuppelt_-_The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects.djvu/83" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:Ruppelt - The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects.djvu/83 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

## What explanations remain plausible

The leading explanation today is that many green fireball reports were caused by unusually bright meteors, also known as fireballs or bolides. Meteors can appear green when materials in the incoming object or the surrounding atmosphere produce particular wavelengths of light during the intense heating process. Bright meteors can also appear to move in unusual ways because human estimates of distance, speed and trajectory are difficult during short, dramatic events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/twinkle/ProjectTwinkleFinalReport.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Twinkle Final Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I &amp; B &gt; 2 P 5* o 0. » UNCLASSIFIED c&gt;...</span></span></span>

The historical record also shows why the mystery persisted. A normal meteor explanation did not immediately satisfy every witness or investigator because some reports contained features that seemed unusual: repeated sightings in one region, apparent slow movement, vivid colour and the absence of recovered fragments. Those gaps encouraged speculation, especially because the events occurred near locations associated with the most secret scientific programme of the age.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/gfb/rufos4u.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: The Report On Unidentified Flying Objects - Chapter Four Unedited...</span></span></span>

Other conventional possibilities were also considered. Investigators examined whether some sightings might involve aircraft, rockets, balloons or other human-made objects. The early Cold War made those possibilities important because both the United States and the Soviet Union were developing advanced military technologies that were not publicly visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/twinkle/ProjectTwinkleFinalReport.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Twinkle Final Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I &amp; B &gt; 2 P 5* o 0. » UNCLASSIFIED c&gt;...</span></span></span>

The enduring significance of the green fireballs is therefore not that they provide evidence of unknown technology, but that they show how uncertainty works around sensitive sites. Near nuclear facilities, unexplained observations were treated as potential security issues first and scientific puzzles second. The episode helped shape early [UFO investigations]({{ 'ufo-case-investigation/' | relative_url }}) by creating a model that would be repeated for later reports near military and nuclear locations: gather witnesses, collect measurements, test ordinary explanations, and preserve uncertainty only where evidence remains incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span></span></span>

The green fireball cases remain one of the clearest examples of the intersection between [Cold War secrecy]({{ 'cold-war-secrecy/' | relative_url }}), nuclear-era anxiety and scientific investigation. They were unusual enough to involve leading researchers and government agencies, yet the surviving evidence points most strongly toward natural aerial phenomena rather than a hidden connection to nuclear weapons or unknown technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/twinkle/ProjectTwinkleFinalReport.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Twinkle Final Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I &amp; B &gt; 2 P 5* o 0. » UNCLASSIFIED c&gt;...</span></span></span>
