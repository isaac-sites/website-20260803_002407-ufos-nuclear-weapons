## Introduction

The green fireballs reported over New Mexico near Los Alamos in 1948–1949 were investigated partly because a natural explanation — unusually bright meteors — could account for many of their visible characteristics. A meteor, especially a large one known as a fireball or bolide, can produce an intense flash of light, unusual colours, and an apparently strange path across the sky when viewed from the ground. These effects made meteors a serious candidate explanation for some of the reports, even though investigators at the time found certain observations difficult to reconcile with ordinary meteor behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020</span></span></span>

The meteor explanation does not require unusual technology or unknown aircraft. It relies on well-understood atmospheric physics: a small object from space enters the atmosphere at high speed, heats the surrounding air into glowing plasma, and produces a bright trail whose colour depends on both the object’s composition and the gases it excites. The challenge in the Los Alamos cases was not whether meteors could appear green, but whether the particular combination of brightness, colour, trajectory, and timing matched what observers were seeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prc.nao.ac.jp/extra/uos/en/no09/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prc.nao.ac.jp">[国立天文台 天文情報センター]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prc.nao.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">国立天文台 天文情報センターSpectra of the Moon &#124; Universe of Spectroscopy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 19, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2012</span></span></span>

## How bright meteors appear in the sky

A fireball begins as a meteoroid — a fragment of rock or metal travelling through space — entering Earth’s atmosphere at high velocity. The visible glow is produced mainly by the heated gas around the object and by atoms from both the meteoroid and the atmosphere emitting light as they become excited. Spectroscopic studies of meteors show that common contributors include elements such as magnesium, sodium, iron, calcium, and atmospheric oxygen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prc.nao.ac.jp/extra/uos/en/no09/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prc.nao.ac.jp">[国立天文台 天文情報センター]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prc.nao.ac.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">国立天文台 天文情報センターSpectra of the Moon &#124; Universe of Spectroscopy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 19, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2012</span></span></span>

Green coloration is therefore not an unusual feature by itself. Bright meteors can appear green because of specific emissions from the material being vaporised and from the surrounding atmosphere. Fast meteors can strongly excite atmospheric oxygen, while metallic elements released from the meteoroid can also contribute to the observed colour. Modern meteor spectroscopy has confirmed that fireball colours are produced by a mixture of these physical processes rather than by a single simple cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15793" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

This mechanism helps explain why eyewitness descriptions of “green balls of fire” do not automatically indicate something beyond normal astronomy. A large meteor viewed at night can look surprisingly solid, bright, and controlled because human observers have difficulty judging distance, altitude, and speed from a single point of view. A long, shallow atmospheric path can also make a meteor appear to travel horizontally rather than falling steeply downward.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

## Comparing meteor and UFO reports

The Los Alamos green fireball reports became controversial because they occurred in an unusually sensitive setting. Reports came from the region surrounding nuclear research facilities, and military investigators considered whether the objects represented a security concern. The Air Force investigation consulted meteor specialists, including astronomers who studied meteorites, and presented findings to [scientists]({{ 'scientists/' | relative_url }}) connected with Los Alamos and other government organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020</span></span></span>

Several features of the sightings supported a meteor interpretation. Witnesses frequently described brilliant green objects crossing the sky rapidly, which is consistent with bright atmospheric fireballs. Some reports also resembled known meteor events, including observations of large green fireballs by aircraft crews in December 1948. Later summaries of the investigation noted that greenish meteors and unusual viewing angles could explain some of the characteristics that initially seemed extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

However, investigators did not immediately accept the meteor explanation for every report. Astronomer Lincoln LaPaz, who played a major role in analysing the sightings, argued that some features did not fit conventional meteor observations, including aspects of the reported trajectories and appearance. The 1949 Los Alamos conference brought together scientists and military officials to examine competing explanations, with meteors among the leading natural hypotheses but not a complete explanation for every observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://warufo.com/document/295" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: warufo.com">[WARUFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">warufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DOE-UAP-D004, Los Alamos Conference on Aerial Phenomena, 1949 — WARUFO — Declassified UAP Document Archive...</span></span></span>

The uncertainty came largely from limitations of the evidence. Most observations were visual reports rather than measurements from instruments. Without photographs, accurate trajectory data, or recovered material, investigators had to estimate speed, distance, altitude, and size from human perception. ## Why some sightings stayed uncertain

The green fireball investigations illustrate a broader difficulty in analysing unusual lights in the sky: appearance alone rarely identifies the cause. A witness can accurately report what they saw while still being unable to determine the object’s true nature. Brightness, colour, and apparent movement are influenced by atmospheric conditions, viewing angle, and the observer’s location.

The Los Alamos cases remained interesting because the meteor explanation was plausible but difficult to prove for individual events. Some sightings likely represented ordinary or unusual meteors, while others lacked enough information for investigators to confidently classify them. The Air Force’s continued study reflected this uncertainty rather than a conclusion that the objects were necessarily extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Special Investigations Project Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; Display</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020</span></span></span>
