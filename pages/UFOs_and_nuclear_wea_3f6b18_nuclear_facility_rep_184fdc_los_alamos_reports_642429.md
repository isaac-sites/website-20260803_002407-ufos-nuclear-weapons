## Introduction

Los Alamos became one of the earliest and most significant locations in UFO history because it combined two unusual circumstances: a highly secret nuclear research environment and a series of unexplained aerial reports over the American Southwest. The best-documented episode was the late-1940s “green fireball” phenomenon, in which bright green objects were reported over areas connected to the US atomic programme, prompting investigations involving military intelligence officers, [scientists]({{ 'scientists/' | relative_url }}) and researchers associated with Los Alamos. These events show less that UFOs posed a proven extraordinary threat and more how governments handled uncertainty around a facility where any unexplained activity could have security implications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2020</span></span></span>

The Los Alamos cases remain important because they sit at the intersection of science, secrecy and national security. Officials did not treat the reports simply as curiosities: they considered whether the objects represented natural phenomena, [foreign technology]({{ 'foreign-risks/' | relative_url }}), experimental systems or some other unknown cause. However, decades of investigations did not produce publicly verified evidence that the sightings represented [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) technology or a direct threat to nuclear operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.secretsdeclassified.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/459832/project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretsdeclassified.af.mil">[Secrets Declassified]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretsdeclassified.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

## Atomic research setting and secrecy

### Why Los Alamos drew official attention

Los Alamos was central to the Manhattan Project and later US [nuclear weapons]({{ 'nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}) research, making it one of the most sensitive scientific locations in the world. In the early Cold War, uncertainty around anything observed near nuclear facilities carried greater weight than an ordinary aerial sighting because of concerns about espionage, surveillance and technological competition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05727" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nuclear Science for the Manhattan Project and Comparison to Today&#x27;s ENDF DataMarch 9, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2021</span></span></span>

The late-1940s sightings occurred during a period when American officials were already concerned about protecting nuclear knowledge and monitoring potential Soviet capabilities. The surrounding region contained several strategically important installations, including facilities connected with atomic weapons development and military research. Contemporary Air Force investigations noted that unusual aerial activity in the area was significant precisely because it occurred near sensitive defence locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

This security environment shaped how reports were interpreted. A strange object near a civilian area might be dismissed as a misidentification, but a similar observation near Los Alamos raised questions about whether it involved reconnaissance, a secret aircraft programme or a failure to understand a natural event. The concern was therefore not only “what was seen?” but also “could an unknown object reveal a vulnerability around critical infrastructure?”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2020</span></span></span>

## Reported sightings and investigations

### The green fireball investigations of 1948–1950

The most notable Los Alamos-related UFO reports involved green fireballs observed across New Mexico beginning in 1948. Witnesses described bright green objects moving across the sky, often with unusual appearances compared with ordinary meteors. The reports attracted attention from Air Force intelligence units, which worked with scientists to determine whether the phenomenon had a conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2020</span></span></span>

One major scientific figure involved was meteor expert Lincoln LaPaz of the University of New Mexico. Military investigators consulted LaPaz because one possibility was that the objects were unusual meteoric events. The investigation expanded because the observations did not immediately fit simple explanations, leading to broader study involving military agencies and scientists connected with New Mexico research institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2020</span></span></span>

A February 1949 conference at Los Alamos brought together scientists and military representatives to discuss the phenomenon. Historical accounts of the investigation describe participation by figures including Edward Teller and other researchers associated with nuclear science, reflecting the seriousness with which officials examined unexplained events near strategic facilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The investigations eventually contributed to further Air Force study, including Project TWINKLE, which examined green fireball reports in the Southwest. The existence of these programmes demonstrates that the sightings were treated as a genuine investigative issue, but official investigations did not establish that they were hostile craft or evidence of non-human technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/15" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/15 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

### What the investigations actually established

The strongest historical conclusion is that Los Alamos was the site of serious government interest in unexplained aerial observations, not that the site experienced confirmed UFO incursions. Investigators collected witness reports, consulted specialists and explored multiple explanations. The available records show uncertainty and investigation rather than proof of an extraordinary cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.osi.af.mil/News/Features/Display/Article/2302429/project-blue-book-part-1-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: osi.af.mil">[Office of Special Investigations]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">osi.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Special InvestigationsProject Blue Book Part 1 (UFO Reports) &gt; Office of Special Investigations &gt; DisplayAugust 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 6, 2020</span></span></span>

## Security concerns and interpretations

### Why unexplained objects mattered at a nuclear laboratory

The security importance of the Los Alamos reports came from the location, not from confirmed evidence of danger. During the early nuclear era, the United States had strong incentives to identify anything unusual near atomic research sites. An unidentified object could theoretically have represented surveillance, a test of foreign technology or an unknown military system, even if the final explanation proved ordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Report_on_Unidentified_Flying_Objects/Chapter_4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects/Chapter 4 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

### Security history versus UFO mythology

Los Alamos occupies a prominent place in UFO discussions partly because secrecy surrounded the laboratory. Classified nuclear research, limited public information and Cold War tensions created conditions where unexplained events could generate speculation. However, secrecy itself does not demonstrate that a hidden extraordinary explanation existed. It often reflected the normal protection of military and scientific programmes during the period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/roswell-aliens-fermi-paradox" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Here&#x27;s the Proof There&#x27;s No Government Alien Conspiracy Around Roswell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">However, an examination reveals a confluence of secret government projects and Cold War era activities rather than extraterrestrial invol...</span></span></span>

The broader lesson from the Los Alamos reports is about governance under uncertainty. Officials had to balance openness, scientific investigation and protection of sensitive facilities.
