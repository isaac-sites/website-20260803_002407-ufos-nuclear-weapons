## How Hastings built his nuclear UFO case

Hastings began investigating UFO reports in the early 1970s and gradually shifted his focus towards incidents involving military nuclear facilities. His central method has been interviewing former military personnel who reported unusual events while working at missile sites, weapons storage areas, or nuclear-related installations. ## The Malmstrom missile shutdown claims

The most famous cases in Hastings’ research involve Malmstrom Air Force Base in 1967. Former Air Force missile officers, especially [Robert Salas]({{ 'robert-salas/' | relative_url }}), have described incidents in which missile systems reportedly became non-operational while security personnel observed unusual lights or objects near launch facilities. Hastings made these accounts central to his argument that UFOs may have interacted with nuclear weapons systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohastings.com/interviews" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohastings.com">[UFO Hastings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohastings.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span></span></span>

According to the witness accounts collected by Hastings, one March 1967 incident involved multiple Minuteman missiles becoming unavailable for launch while a reddish aerial object was reportedly seen near the base. Hastings has interpreted these events as possible demonstrations of technological capability directed at nuclear weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohastings.com/interviews" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohastings.com">[UFO Hastings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohastings.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span></span></span>

## The 2010 National Press Club event

A major milestone in Hastings’ research was the 27 September 2010 “UFOs and Nukes” press conference in Washington, DC. The event brought together Hastings and several retired Air Force personnel who described alleged UFO incidents connected with nuclear weapons facilities. The event received significant media attention, including a live stream by CNN.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohastings.com/book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohastings.com">[UFO Hastings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohastings.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span></span></span>

## The main criticisms of Hastings’ interpretation

Critics of Hastings’ work generally do not dispute that military personnel reported unusual incidents. Instead, they challenge the conclusions drawn from those reports.

One criticism is that witness testimony collected decades after events may be affected by memory changes, later interpretations, or the influence of existing UFO narratives. Another is that unusual technical failures at complex military facilities may have conventional explanations that are difficult to reconstruct from incomplete public records.

The broader government review of historical UAP claims has also produced a more cautious assessment. AARO’s 2024 historical report concluded that it found no evidence that US government investigations had confirmed UFO sightings as extraterrestrial technology. It also found no empirical evidence supporting claims of recovered extraterrestrial technology or [reverse engineering]({{ 'reverse-engineering/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

These findings do not directly resolve every individual nuclear-related UFO report, but they illustrate the difference between documenting unexplained events and demonstrating an extraordinary explanation.

## Why Hastings’ research remains influential

Regardless of whether readers accept Hastings’ conclusions, his work changed the structure of the nuclear UFO debate. Before his research became widely known, many UFO discussions focused on civilian sightings, photographs, and individual encounters. Hastings shifted attention towards military testimony and the question of whether unexplained aerial events appeared around some of the world’s most sensitive strategic locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohastings.com/book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohastings.com">[UFO Hastings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohastings.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hastings UFOs &amp; Nukes</span></span></span>

The continuing interest in his work comes from the unusual setting of the cases. Reports involving nuclear missile facilities naturally attract attention because these sites involve national security, highly trained personnel, and systems designed to prevent failures. The unresolved issue is not whether nuclear-related UFO reports exist as historical claims, but what those reports ultimately represent: unknown technology, misidentified events, security incidents, technical failures, or something else still not demonstrated by public evidence.

Hastings’ contribution is therefore best understood as a major body of case collection and testimony within UFO research. It provides a detailed record of claims surrounding nuclear facilities while leaving the central question of cause and explanation open to continued investigation.
