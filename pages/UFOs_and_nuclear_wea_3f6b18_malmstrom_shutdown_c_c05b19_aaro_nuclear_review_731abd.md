## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) reviewed several historical claims that unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) were connected to [nuclear weapons]({{ 'nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}) systems, including the widely discussed Malmstrom Air Force Base missile shutdown accounts. Its review did not verify that UAP caused [missile failures]({{ 'nuclear-missile/' | relative_url }}) or that unknown craft interfered with nuclear operations. Instead, AARO concluded that the available evidence consisted mainly of retrospective witness accounts, with limited surviving technical data capable of proving a physical connection between reported objects and missile events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

The review matters because Malmstrom and similar nuclear-related cases occupy a unique place in UAP discussions: they involve military personnel, strategic weapons, and claims of systems being affected. AARO’s examination shifted the central question from whether a dramatic story existed to whether there was sufficient evidence to establish causation. Its assessment found that the historical record did not demonstrate that UAP were responsible for nuclear missile disruptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327/pdf/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB PREX28 PURL gpo223327</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1June 26, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2025</span></span></span>

## Government examination of reports

AARO’s Historical Record Report, Volume I, examined claims that UAP had appeared near nuclear facilities and affected missile operations. The office specifically identified a group of historical narratives involving U.S. intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) sites, including accounts associated with Malmstrom Air Force Base in Montana, Ellsworth Air Force Base in South Dakota, Vandenberg Air Force Base in California, and Minot Air Force Base in North Dakota.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

The nuclear-related claims reviewed by AARO generally followed a similar pattern. Former personnel described unusual lights or objects near missile facilities and linked those sightings to operational problems such as launch control facilities going offline or missile systems becoming unavailable. Some accounts also involved claims that UAP affected missile tests or destroyed a test vehicle. AARO treated these as historical allegations requiring examination rather than as established events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/30" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/30 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

For the [Malmstrom claims]({{ 'malmstrom-claims/' | relative_url }}), this distinction was especially important. The underlying missile incidents were real historical events: missile systems experienced failures and required investigation. The unresolved issue was whether a UAP was involved. AARO’s review found that the evidence available decades later did not establish that link. A malfunction, even one occurring close in time to a reported sighting, was not considered proof that an external object caused the failure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)

AARO also examined broader allegations that secret programmes or hidden technologies explained nuclear-related UAP reports. In its wider historical review, the office reported finding no empirical evidence that U.S. government programmes had recovered or reverse-engineered [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) technology. Some accounts, AARO said, appeared to involve misunderstandings of genuine classified or advanced technology programmes rather than evidence of non-human systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

## Interviews with former personnel

A key part of AARO’s approach was interviewing former military personnel who had direct or claimed direct connections to nuclear-related incidents. The office interviewed five former U.S. Air Force members who served in or around ICBM facilities between 1966 and 1977. Their accounts covered reported UAP sightings near missile sites, alleged disruptions to missile operations, and related claims involving missile tests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

These interviews were significant because many nuclear UAP stories rely heavily on first-person testimony. Military witnesses are often viewed by supporters of these claims as particularly credible because of their training and responsibilities. AARO did not dismiss the witnesses themselves; rather, it assessed whether their memories and interpretations could be matched with records, technical information, and other evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

In several cases, AARO was able to identify real people, programmes, or activities that corresponded with elements of the stories it investigated. The office reported that some narratives appeared to combine authentic national-security activities with later interpretations that connected them to UAP.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

This evidence-assessment method is particularly relevant to Malmstrom. The accounts involve trained observers describing unusual circumstances, but decades-old recollections can leave important questions unanswered: what exact technical failure occurred, what data was recorded at the time, what objects were observed, and whether the two events were actually connected. Without contemporaneous sensor records, maintenance documentation, or other direct evidence linking a UAP to a missile system, AARO did not consider the causal claim demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)

## Limits of available evidence

The biggest limitation identified in AARO’s review was not whether unusual reports existed, but whether enough evidence remained to test the strongest claims. Nuclear-related UAP stories often involve events from the 1960s and 1970s, meaning investigations rely heavily on memories collected many years after the incidents rather than complete original datasets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)

AARO noted that historical UAP cases frequently lack the type of evidence needed for firm conclusions. In nuclear cases, the most valuable evidence would include original technical records, [sensor data]({{ 'sensor-data/' | relative_url }}), maintenance reports, communications logs, and physical evidence showing a mechanism by which an object affected a weapon system. Much of that material was either unavailable, incomplete, or insufficient to support the extraordinary interpretation of the events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)

The report also addressed a related issue: secrecy surrounding nuclear programmes can make ordinary explanations harder to evaluate. During the Cold War, many advanced military projects were classified, and personnel may have encountered unfamiliar technology without knowing its purpose. AARO found that some historical UAP narratives were linked to real but unrelated national-security activities, creating opportunities for misinterpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/learn/aaro-historical-record-report-findings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro historical record report findings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveWhat the AARO Historical Record Report Found (2024) &#124; UAP Records Archive...</span></span></span>

The unresolved status of some cases does not mean AARO confirmed extraordinary explanations. Instead, it means the remaining evidence was not sufficient to reach a definitive conclusion. For the Malmstrom missile shutdown claims specifically, AARO’s assessment left the documented missile failures intact while rejecting the stronger claim that UAP involvement had been proven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf)

AARO’s review therefore changed the status of nuclear UAP claims in an important way: they moved from being primarily stories about alleged incidents to being cases evaluated through an evidence standard. The historical record shows genuine missile events and genuine reports by military personnel, but it does not provide verified evidence that UAP caused nuclear weapons systems to fail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327/pdf/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB PREX28 PURL gpo223327</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1June 26, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2025</span></span></span>
