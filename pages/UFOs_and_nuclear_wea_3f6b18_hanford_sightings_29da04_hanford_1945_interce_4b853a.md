## Introduction

The 1945 [Hanford]({{ 'hanford/' | relative_url }}) interception claim is one of the earliest stories linking unidentified aerial reports with the atomic weapons programme. The account describes a radar detection over the Hanford Engineering Works in Washington state, followed by an attempted interception by Navy F6F Hellcat pilots from nearby Pasco Naval Air Station. According to later retellings, the pilots saw a large, unusual object at extreme altitude but could not reach it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

## Radar report and pilot accounts

The reported incident is usually dated to July 1945, during the final months of the Second World War. Hanford was then part of the Manhattan Project, producing plutonium for atomic weapons. The later account states that the air defence unit at Pasco Naval Air Station received an alert after radar operators detected a fast-moving object above the Hanford facility. Six Navy pilots were reportedly sent to investigate using Grumman F6F Hellcat fighters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

The central witness is Rolan D. Powell, later a US Navy pilot and civilian test pilot. In accounts published decades after the alleged event, Powell described an object that appeared extremely large, oval-shaped, and difficult to approach. Later versions of his testimony said the object was estimated to be around 65,000 feet above the ground and that the Hellcats exceeded their normal operating limits while attempting to intercept it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

The reported visual description became the most memorable part of the case. Powell was quoted in later UFO publications as comparing the object’s apparent size to “three aircraft carriers side by side” and describing it as a streamlined, pinkish shape with vapour around its edges. The account also claimed that the object remained stationary for a period before disappearing upwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

From an evidence perspective, the radar claim and the pilot testimony are separate issues. If a wartime radar record existed, it would provide an important independent line of evidence. However, the publicly available case history does not present a surviving radar log, Navy report, or operational record that can be examined and verified. The radar element is therefore known mainly through later recollections rather than through an accessible primary document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

## Later UFO literature interpretations

The Hanford story became significant in UFO literature because of its timing. The alleged sighting occurred close to the Trinity nuclear test in July 1945 and at a site involved in producing plutonium for the first atomic weapons. Later writers used this connection to place Hanford among the earliest examples of alleged UFO interest in nuclear facilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

The modern version of the story was developed primarily through UFO researchers and publications rather than through official military channels. A frequently cited source is Byron D. Varner’s privately published book about his aviation experiences, along with later interviews involving Powell and UFO researcher Walter Andrus. The account was subsequently reproduced by organisations and researchers interested in historical UFO cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/acufo/1945-07-00-hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs at close sight: ACUFO catalog - Case of the Hanford nuclear plant, Washington, USA, in July 1945July 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

Supporters of the case argue that several features make it unusual: the reported combination of radar and visual observation, the involvement of trained military pilots, and the location near a highly sensitive atomic facility. They view the case as an early example of a pattern later claimed in UFO literature, where [unidentified objects]({{ 'unidentified-objects/' | relative_url }}) were reported near nuclear-related sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/hanford.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[Nicap]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Over Hanford Nuclear Reactor. 1945...</span></span></span>

Critics focus on the weaknesses of the documentation. The story emerged many years after the alleged event, and the available evidence does not include the kinds of records normally used to verify a wartime aviation incident, such as squadron logs, official correspondence, maintenance records, or contemporaneous witness statements from multiple independent sources. ## Limits of surviving records

The biggest challenge for evaluating the 1945 Hanford interception claim is the gap between the dramatic later narrative and the surviving evidence. Unlike later US Air Force [UFO investigations]({{ 'ufo-case-investigation/' | relative_url }}), which generated extensive files through programmes such as Project Blue Book, the Hanford account predates those formal investigations. Project [Blue Book records]({{ 'blue-book-records/' | relative_url }}) cover investigations from 1947 onwards and do not provide a contemporary official case file for this 1945 event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theprojectbluebookarchive.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theprojectbluebookarchive.org">[Project Blue Book Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theprojectbluebookarchive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book Archive The Project Blue Book Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book Archive The Project Blue Book Archive</span></span></span>
