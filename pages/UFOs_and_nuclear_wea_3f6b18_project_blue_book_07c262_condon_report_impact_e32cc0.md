## Introduction

The Condon Report was the scientific review that effectively ended the United States Air Force’s public [UFO investigation]({{ 'ufo-case-investigation/' | relative_url }}) programme. Conducted by the University of Colorado between 1966 and 1968 under physicist Edward U. Condon, the study concluded that further large-scale UFO research was unlikely to produce significant scientific discoveries. The Air Force used this conclusion, alongside a review by the National Academy of Sciences, as the basis for closing [Project Blue Book]({{ 'project-blue-book/' | relative_url }}) in 1969.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

Its impact was larger than simply ending a government office. The report changed the status of UFO investigations from a Cold War military activity into a largely civilian and controversial research field. Supporters viewed it as the strongest scientific assessment yet that UFO reports did not justify continued federal investigation, while critics argued that the study’s methods and conclusions did not fully resolve the most puzzling cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

## University of Colorado scientific review

### A scientific assessment designed to settle the question

The Condon Report, formally titled *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects*, was commissioned by the Air Force after renewed public pressure over UFO sightings in the mid-1960s. The University of Colorado team examined existing UFO records, investigated selected cases, and reviewed evidence from areas including astronomy, atmospheric science, psychology, and engineering. The project was directed by Edward Condon, a respected physicist who had worked in nuclear physics and was a prominent figure in twentieth-century American science.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/covrletr.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, University of Colorado Submission Letter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, University of Colorado Submission LetterOctober 31, 1968...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 1968</span></span></span>

The report represented an attempt to move UFO investigations away from military speculation and towards a scientific cost-benefit judgement: could systematic study of UFO reports produce useful scientific knowledge? Its central conclusion was negative. Condon wrote that the available record from the previous 21 years had not produced discoveries that advanced science and that further extensive study was unlikely to do so.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

This conclusion directly affected the role of Project Blue Book. Earlier Air Force investigations had been justified partly by national security concerns, especially during the Cold War when unidentified aircraft near military installations could potentially represent [foreign technology]({{ 'foreign-risks/' | relative_url }}). The Condon review argued that the accumulated evidence did not show that UFO reports represented a continuing scientific or defence problem requiring a dedicated programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

### The report’s evidence base and approach

The study examined a wide range of reports rather than focusing only on dramatic incidents. Researchers attempted to separate cases with insufficient information from those that could be explained through conventional causes such as aircraft, astronomical objects, weather effects, or mistakes in observation. The final report included detailed case analyses alongside broader recommendations about whether continued investigation was justified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s5chap02.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Sec V, Chapter 2: UFOs: 1947 - 1968October 31, 1968...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 1968</span></span></span>

A key point in the report’s influence was not that every UFO sighting had been explained. Instead, the argument was that unexplained reports did not automatically provide evidence of extraordinary technology or a phenomenon requiring a permanent government research programme. This distinction became central to later debates over UFO investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

## Findings and disputed conclusions

### Why supporters considered the report decisive

For the Air Force, the Condon Report provided an independent scientific basis for ending Project Blue Book. The service announced the programme’s termination in December 1969, stating that the decision followed evaluation of the University of Colorado report, the National Academy of Sciences review, previous UFO studies, and the Air Force’s own investigation record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The report’s influence came partly from the reputation of the institution involved. Rather than being an internal Air Force investigation, it was conducted by a major university under contract. The Air Force could therefore present closure as a conclusion reached through external scientific evaluation rather than simply a military administrative decision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/covrletr.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, University of Colorado Submission Letter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, University of Colorado Submission LetterOctober 31, 1968...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 1968</span></span></span>

The result was a major change in official policy. After Project Blue Book closed, the Air Force no longer maintained a dedicated public office for investigating UFO reports, and its records were transferred to the National Archives for public access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

### Why critics challenged its conclusions

The report remained controversial among UFO researchers and some [scientists]({{ 'scientists/' | relative_url }}) because critics argued that its overall conclusion did not fully reflect the uncertainty within the case files. Astronomer J. Allen [Hynek]({{ 'hynek/' | relative_url }}), who had served as an Air Force consultant on UFOs for more than two decades, criticised the report’s interpretation and argued that some unresolved cases deserved continued scientific attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/shg/articles/bas1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947SHG - The Condon Report and UFOs: Dr. J. Critics also pointed to tensions within the project itself. A pre-project memorandum by coordinator Robert Low became controversial because some readers interpreted it as showing that investigators expected a negative conclusion before the study was completed. Supporters of the report argued that such expectations did not determine the quality of the research, while critics saw it as evidence of an overly sceptical starting point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/11/26/the-condon-report-a-scientific-study-of-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newspaceeconomy.ca">[New Space Economy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newspaceeconomy.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Space EconomyThe Condon Report: A Scientific Study of UFOs &#124; New Space EconomyNovember 26, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 26, 2025</span></span></span>

## Closure of the Air Force programme

### How the Condon Report ended Project Blue Book

The immediate practical effect of the Condon Report was the closure of Project Blue Book. By the late 1960s, the Air Force had investigated thousands of reports, but officials concluded that the programme had not demonstrated a national security threat, evidence of technology beyond contemporary science, or proof that UFOs represented [extraterrestrial]({{ 'extraterrestrial/' | relative_url }}) vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The termination marked the end of a 22-year period of organised US Air Force UFO investigations. Between 1947 and 1969, Air Force programmes collected thousands of reports, with Project Blue Book becoming the final and longest-running effort. After closure, UFO reports were no longer handled through a specialised public Air Force investigation unit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

In the context of [UFOs and nuclear weapons]({{ 'ufos-and-nuclear-weapons/' | relative_url }}), the Condon Report also reinforced a shift in how unusual aerial reports near military sites were interpreted. Instead of maintaining a separate UFO programme, the government position was that genuine defence concerns could be handled through normal military intelligence and surveillance channels rather than through a dedicated UFO office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

### The lasting impact on UFO research

The report did not end interest in UFOs. Instead, it divided the field. Mainstream scientific institutions largely accepted the view that UFO reports had not produced evidence requiring major research investment, while civilian researchers argued that some cases remained insufficiently studied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>

Its legacy remains tied to a larger question: whether unexplained observations represent a scientific problem, a data-quality problem, or simply a collection of ordinary events that are difficult to identify. The Condon Report answered that question by recommending closure, and that recommendation transformed UFO investigations from an official Air Force responsibility into a continuing public debate over evidence, methodology, and government [transparency]({{ 'transparency/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/sec-i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.ncas.org">[NCAS PDF Directory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.ncas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PDF Directory Condon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NCAS PDF DirectoryCondon Report, Section I: Conclusions &amp; Recommendations...</span></span></span>
